How a project runs

  1. Brief

    We agree on what the board must do: power, size, interfaces and constraints.

  2. Architecture and simulation

    I choose the topology and check it in LTspice or Simulink before drawing the board.

  3. Schematic and layout

    Schematic capture and PCB layout in Altium Designer, with a design review.

  4. Prototype and bring-up

    Assembly, power-up checks and testing against the brief.

  5. Handover

    Fabrication files, BOM, design notes and a bring-up checklist.

Selected work

PSK Gate System

Race-timing gate for athletes at stadium tracks. I owned it end to end at ELKO EP.

The client's earlier system picked up IR interference at sunrise. Switching to a red-LED sensor removed it, and I designed the schematic and 2-layer PCB that runs the new setup.

Two Optex CDD-100N-R sensors per gate feed a logic stage that confirms each crossing. An optocoupler isolates the 12 V sensors from the 3.3 V Raspberry Pi logic.

The sensors and the handheld button connect over USB Type-C into one enclosure, so each one can be unplugged and replaced.

I took it from prototype through client validation, then assembled 12 units in 3 weeks. All 12 work, and the client has asked for further development.

Dual-axis solar tracker

Project at NTI. Simulink and Altium Designer.

I modelled the full power chain in Simulink: PV panel, CN3791 charger, Li-ion protection and a 5 V boost stage, together with the tracking control. It runs as a model-in-the-loop simulation of a small tracker with a 6 W panel, a single 3 Ah Li-ion cell and a 5 V load.

I also designed the 2-layer PCB around the same chain, with an INA219 for monitoring and an ATmega328P as the controller.
